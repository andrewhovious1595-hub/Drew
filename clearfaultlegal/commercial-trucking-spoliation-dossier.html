1. The Electronic Control Module (ECM) & Black Box Stack

Commercial tractors equipped with Detroit Diesel (DDEC), Cummins, Caterpillar, or PACCAR powertrains record sudden deceleration events (Hard Brake Events) on separate internal non-volatile memory partitions. Data channels include:

• Pre-Incident Vehicle Speed Sampled second-by-second across the 60 seconds prior to collision lock.
• Engine RPM & Gear Selection Identifies engine overspeed, jake-brake engagement, and transmission status.
• Clutch & Brake Switch States Proves exact millisecond driver reaction time vs. visual line-of-sight.
• Forward Collision Warning Radar Target tracking, lead vehicle distance, and autonomous emergency braking (AEB) alerts.

2. Auditing Electronic Logging Devices (ELD) Under 49 CFR § 395

Driver fatigue is a factor in over 30% of commercial carrier fatalities. Under 49 CFR Part 395, motor carriers must maintain raw ELD output files. ClearFault Legal cross-references raw ELD GPS waypoints against cell tower records, bill of lading time-stamps, and toll transponder scans to expose:

  • Ghost Driving & Off-Duty Tampering: Driving in "Personal Conveyance" or "Yard Move" modes to conceal 11-hour driving limit violations.
  • Coerced Dispatch: Route schedules mathematically impossible to complete within lawful speed limits and 14-hour duty windows.
  • 30-Minute Rest Break Omissions: Failure to take consecutive mandatory off-duty rest breaks before operating in congested corridors.

3. Heavy Vehicle Stopping Distance & Thermal Brake Fade Modeling

A fully loaded 80,000-pound commercial semi-truck traveling at 65 MPH requires approximately 525 feet (nearly two football fields) to come to a complete stop under optimal conditions. When out-of-adjustment S-cam drum brakes or glazed brake linings experience thermal fade, stopping distances expand exponentially.
