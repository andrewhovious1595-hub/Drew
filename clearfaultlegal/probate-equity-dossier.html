CALIFORNIA PROBATE CODE INVESTIGATIVE BRIEF

California Probate Real Estate & Estate Equity Preservation

A forensic guide to defending contested real property equity against predatory receiverships, below-market wholesale liquidations, and undue influence under California Probate Code § 850.

Published by ClearFault Legal Research • Updated: November 2026 • Fresno, California Jurisdiction

Executive Summary

Real estate is frequently the single largest asset in a California probate estate. Disputed titles, predatory co-heir disputes, and receivership motions often trigger catastrophic equity loss through court-ordered auction discounts (often 20% to 35% below fair market value). By deploying early statutory remedies—specifically Probate Code § 850 petitions coupled with recorded notices of pendency of action (Lis Pendens) and the California Partition of Real Property Act (Code of Civil Procedure § 874.311 et seq.)—heirs and estates can halt forced sales, secure appraisal floors, and recover elder-financial-abuse treble damages under Welfare & Institutions Code § 15610.30.

1. The Mechanics of Probate Code § 850 Petitions

California Probate Code § 850 (commonly known as an "850 Petition") empowers the personal representative or any interested person to petition the court to determine title to personal or real property when the decedent died in possession of, or holding title to, property claimed by another—or conversely, where the decedent held an equitable claim to property titled in another person's name.

Under § 859, if the court finds that a party has in bad faith wrongfully taken, concealed, or disposed of property belonging to a conservatee, minor, or decedent's estate, the court must hold the wrongful party liable for twice the value of the property recovered (statutory double damages), plus reasonable attorney's fees and costs.

2. Shielding Inherited Real Property Under the Partition Act

Prior to the enactment of the California Partition of Real Property Act, any single co-tenant could force a public sheriff's auction of family property, often resulting in developers acquiring generational assets at distressed prices.

  • Mandatory Fair Market Value Appraisal: CCP § 874.316 requires a court-appointed disinterested appraiser to establish the evidentiary baseline value.
  • Right of First Refusal Buyout: Under CCP § 874.317, non-selling co-owners possess statutory right of first buyout before any open-market listing can occur.
  • Broker Listing vs. Auction: If property must be sold, it must be listed with a licensed real estate broker at fair market value rather than a forced auction.

3. Elder Financial Abuse Restitution Strategies

When real property deeds are conveyed prior to death under circumstances involving undue influence or cognitive decline, California Welfare & Institutions Code § 15610.30 operates in tandem with Probate Code § 850.
